Auzietek helps teams turn fragile infrastructure work into cleaner systems, repeatable operations, and evidence-backed automation.

Auzietek is an emerging venture being built in the open from real lab work: BlackKnightController, Linux field notes, retro-computing preservation, and the larger ThinkTank ideas that explain why this work matters. The formal company shape is still forming, but the technical evidence is not theoretical.

That distinction matters. This site should build trust without pretending to be larger than it is today: the work is active, the examples are real, the service direction is serious, and the operating model is still intentionally lean while the business foundation catches up.

The practical promise is simple:

```text
messy system
  -> mapped resources
  -> repeatable procedure
  -> validation
  -> receipt
  -> reusable knowledge
```

## The problem Auzietek is built around

Most infrastructure pain is not caused by one missing tool. It comes from scattered context:

- shell history on one workstation;
- undocumented firewall and DNS decisions;
- dashboards that do not know what changed;
- pipelines disconnected from the hardware they affect;
- rebuild steps that live only in someone’s memory;
- automation that cannot explain its own result.

Auzietek’s work is to make those systems legible again.

## BlackKnightController as product proof

BlackKnightController is the working proof line. It models infrastructure as resources, relationships, actions, fragments, pipelines, and receipts.

![BlackKnightController Company Mind resource graph](/content-files/assets/bkc/bkc-company-mind-resources.png)

That matters because a host is not only a hostname. It has power state, BIOS state, network position, storage layout, service role, monitoring history, and procedures that are safe only under certain conditions.

When the resource graph, pipeline steps, and validation receipts stay close together, the next operator does not have to rediscover the same environment from scratch.

## What we can help with

### Practical advisory and implementation

For the right small team, founder, lab, MSP, or engineering group, Auzietek can help turn fragile operational work into something easier to repeat and explain. That may look like a cleanup engagement, a documented deployment pattern, a proof-of-concept, a migration runbook, or an internal tool that removes recurring toil.

### Infrastructure recovery and cleanup

Recover from drift, damaged workstations, partial migrations, stale DNS, mystery services, and fragile manual procedures. The output should not be a heroic one-off repair. The output should be a known-good path the team can reuse.

### Bare-metal and lab automation

Turn IPMI, PXE, DHCP, DNS, hypervisors, storage, edge routing, and service checks into repeatable flows.

![BlackKnightController pipeline workbench](/content-files/assets/bkc/bkc-pipeline-workbench.png)

### Virtualization and container move-in

Help teams rationalize Proxmox, ESXi, OpenStack, Docker Swarm, K3s, and edge services without pretending one control plane owns every truth.

### Operational documentation

Produce documentation from real work: screenshots, diagrams, command examples, pipeline receipts, and validation checks. The goal is teaching material that says “we did this,” not “someone on the internet says this might work.”

### Human-led AIOps

Use AI assistance carefully: stable tool contracts, explicit permissions, useful context retrieval, and receipts after action. The human keeps intent and boundaries. The system helps preserve memory, reduce repeated discovery, and validate outcomes.

## Why this is different

Auzietek is not trying to make engineering more ceremonial. The point is to make serious systems easier to reason about.

Good automation should reduce the size of the problem. Good documentation should keep evidence near the decision. Good AI assistance should make operators more capable, not more confused.

If the venture becomes the company it is aiming to be, that practical standard stays central: merit over credential theater, remote-first where it makes sense, healthy work practices, ethical automation, and a share of success directed back toward education, disaster relief, community labs, and broader access to advanced tools.

That is the center of the work:

> Solutions should be simpler than the problems that demanded them.
